# Organizational Chart and Operating Model

Planned structure for January 2026.

## Decision forums

### Weekly executive meeting

Participants: CEO and leaders of the four areas. Covers metrics, cross-functional blockers, cash position, and urgent decisions. Maximum duration: 90 minutes.

### Monthly business review

Participants: leadership and metric owners. Compares actuals vs. budget, and discusses acquisition, activation, retention, product, and people.

### Quarterly board meeting

Reviews strategy, risks, financial performance, and capital needs. Board decisions do not replace the executive team's operational responsibility.

### Biweekly product committee

Prioritizes problems and outcomes, not a request list. Product decides the solution; commercial and customer areas provide evidence and impact.

### Monthly security committee

Reviews access controls, vulnerabilities, incidents, critical vendors, and remediation plans.

## Decision rights

- pricing and packaging: CRO proposes, CFO validates impact, and CEO approves;
- roadmap: VP of Product and Technology decides after executive discussion;
- hiring planned in budget: area leader and People approve;
- hiring outside budget: CFO and CEO approve;
- discount above 15%: CRO and CFO;
- critical incident: security lead coordinates response and CEO handles external communication;
- integration sunset: Product proposes and Executive Committee approves the transition plan.

## Structure target by the end of 2026

Up to 62 people, conditional on revenue and quarterly milestones:

- Product and Technology: 23;
- Revenue: 18;
- Customers: 14;
- Finance and Operations: six;
- CEO: one.

Roles are not opened just because they appear in the annual budget. Each quarter has revenue, capacity, and productivity triggers.

## Operating principles

- each metric has one clearly identified owner;
- relevant decisions are documented in writing;
- meetings are for deciding, not for discovering basic context;
- cross-functional problems should not be turned into local goal disputes;
- financial safety takes precedence over speed when there is irreversible risk;
- leaders present variances with cause, impact, and next action.
