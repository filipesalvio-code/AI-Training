# Risks, scenarios, and response plans

Strategic register for quarterly review. Probability and impact use a low, medium, or high scale.

## Risk 1 — banking integration unavailable

**Probability:** medium.  
**Impact:** high.

A change of provider or bank may interrupt synchronization and compromise activation.

Signals: increase in failures, delayed returns, and a drop in renewed connections.

Response: keep OFX import functional, monitor by institution, communicate limitations, and avoid exclusive dependence on a single aggregator.

## Risk 2 — growth outside the customer profile

**Probability:** medium.  
**Impact:** medium/high.

Revenue pressure may bring in complex operations that consume product and support resources.

Signals: exceptions in proposals, customizations, implementation timelines above 60 days, and negative margin per account.

Response: qualification criteria, proportional pricing, and executive approval for exceptions.

## Risk 3 — security or privacy incident

**Probability:** low/medium.  
**Impact:** very high.

Response: strong authentication, least privilege, testing, vendor management, an incident response plan, and periodic drills. Growth does not reduce security requirements.

## Risk 4 — activation does not improve

**Probability:** medium.  
**Impact:** high.

If the new onboarding only changes the interface without solving data and process issues, retention will remain limited.

Response: measure stages, observe implementations, separate product, service, and qualification issues, and test with small cohorts.

## Risk 5 — competitor lowers price

**Probability:** high.  
**Impact:** medium.

Response: avoid a price war, reinforce value for teams, review packaging, and accept losing low-fit customers. Defensive discounting requires a time limit and margin analysis.

## Risk 6 — dependence on key people

**Probability:** medium.  
**Impact:** medium/high.

Response: record decisions, distribute access, document critical routines, and develop successors. No financial process should depend on personal credentials without contingency.

## Base scenario

- gross revenue of R$ 12.6 million;
- balanced growth between direct sales, expansion, and partners;
- gross margin of 78%;
- EBITDA of R$ 1.34 million;
- cash remains above R$ 3 million throughout the year.

Decision: execute hiring based on quarterly triggers.

## Conservative scenario

Assumptions:

- gross revenue of R$ 10.8 million;
- net retention remains at 103%;
- partner program is delayed by two quarters;
- infrastructure cost is 10% above forecast.

Actions:

- freeze positions not yet opened;
- reduce media spend with unproven return;
- preserve security, support, and activation projects;
- renegotiate non-critical contracts;
- review the budget monthly.

Do not cut support linearly, as this may increase churn.

## Expansion scenario

Assumptions:

- gross revenue above R$ 14 million;
- activation above 80%;
- partners generate more than 20% of new revenue;
- acquisition payback period below 11 months.

Actions:

- bring forward hiring for implementation and platform engineering;
- increase investment in the channel with quality control;
- reinforce infrastructure and security;
- test regional expansion before adding new segments.

Exceeding revenue without activation and retention does not trigger the expansion scenario.

## Early warning indicators

- activation by cohort;
- churn in the first 90 days;
- implementation time;
- tickets per integration;
- average discount;
- margin per plan;
- cash runway;
- supplier concentration;
- incidents and near misses;
- overtime hours per team.

## Accountability

Each risk has an owner, but the CFO maintains the register and enforces updates. A risk without a plan, date, and owner is not considered addressed.
