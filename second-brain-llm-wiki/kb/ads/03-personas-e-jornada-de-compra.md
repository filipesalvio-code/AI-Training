# Personas and the buying journey

Personas are communication archetypes, not real people.

## Fernanda — finance manager

Fernanda is 38 years old and leads a team of three people. She is responsible for month-end closing, cash flow, and presenting figures to leadership.

**Goals:** reduce rework, close the month with confidence, create approval rules, and anticipate cash shortages.

**Pain points:** scattered data, inconsistent categories, forgotten collections, and dependence on manually maintained spreadsheets.

**Objections:** fear of losing history, a lengthy implementation, team resistance to the new process, and bank integration not working.

**Helpful content:** migration checklist, cash flow walkthrough, reconciliation use case, and explanation of permissions.

Fernanda is the primary user and usually leads the evaluation.

## Marcelo — business owner

Marcelo is 45 years old and runs a services company with 40 employees. He doesn’t operate finance daily, but he tracks balances, receivables, and obligations.

**Goals:** know how much he can invest, reduce surprises, and monitor the company without asking for a new spreadsheet.

**Pain points:** numbers arrive late, visibility is overly technical, and it is hard to separate economic results from bank balance.

**Objections:** price, team time, and uncertainty about whether the company is already big enough for an ERP.

**Helpful content:** rework cost calculator, executive dashboard, and spreadsheet-vs-system comparison.

Marcelo usually approves the budget.

## Caio — financial analyst

Caio is 27 years old and handles payments, settlement entries, collections, and reconciliation. He knows details the manager does not always see.

**Goals:** reduce repetitive data entry, find entries quickly, and avoid duplicate billing.

**Pain points:** inconsistent OFX, manual tasks, corrections without history, and urgent requests via message.

**Objections:** the system seeming simple during sales but being slow in operation; insufficient shortcuts; permissions blocking work.

**Helpful content:** short routine video, help center, shortcut list, and demo with realistic volume.

Caio influences the decision and can support or block adoption.

## Helena — external accountant

Helena serves multiple companies and receives documents at the end of the month.

**Goals:** receive classified information, access reports, and reduce correction requests.

**Pain points:** categories without accounting linkage, missing documents, and changes after closing.

**Objections:** excessive access, incompatible export, and unclear responsibility between ERP and accounting.

**Helpful content:** Accountant profile, period closing, and accounting export.

Helena is an influencer, not necessarily the buyer.

## Journey summary

### Awareness

The person recognizes the problem but may search for spreadsheet, cash control, or reconciliation—not for the word ERP.

Good assets: diagnosis, educational content, method comparison, and pain-centered ad.

### Consideration

They compare systems and ask about banks, implementation, security, and import.

Good assets: demo, migration guide, product tour, and segment-specific page.

### Decision

They need to validate price, effort, integrations, and team involvement.

Good assets: clear proposal, implementation plan, proof of concept, and objection handling.

### Adoption

The customer needs to register accounts, import history, and adjust the process.

Good assets: first-30-days checklist, role-based training, and activation messages.

### Expansion

After the basic routine, they may adopt approvals, cost centers, boletos, and API.

Good assets: advanced use cases and maturity assessment.
